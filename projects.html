AI Trustworthiness Cue Taxonomy

Design Project (CS F377), BITS Pilani · Mentors: Prof. Siddharth Mehrotra (BITS Pilani), Prof. Pascal König (VU Amsterdam)
Trustworthy AI Black-Box Auditing NIST AI RMF User Study
  • Designed a two-layer taxonomy of AI trust cues (scores, badges, explanations, disclaimers) across finance, healthcare and content moderation. Each cue is traced back to NIST AI RMF properties through shared metric families, then classified on 6 dimensions and by the stakeholder who receives it.
  • Black-box audited the loan-eligibility calculators of 9 Indian lenders and loan marketplaces (including Bajaj Finserv, Kotak and Axis). Developed a repeatable method that recovers each calculator's rule from its probe log alone. It showed all 9 are fixed formulas (1,507 of 1,507 probes matched within ₹1), and the results are published on an open comparison website.
  • Built and deployed an anonymous online user study in which participants compare 9 anonymised lenders' results for their own figures, then rate trust before and after the hidden assumptions are revealed. Answers map onto the taxonomy's role × expertise × trust-propensity layer, and trust is measured with a validated scale.

Mechanistic Auditing of Covert LLM Backdoors via Activation Steering

Collaborative project · white-box probe for a Stackelberg audit game
TransformerLens LoRA / PEFT Activation Steering Mech Interp
  • Built a white-box audit pipeline on Pythia-410m using TransformerLens: injected LoRA backdoors (de novo and latent-amplification variants), computed difference-in-means steering vectors across 9 transformer layers, and automated backdoor detection with 0% false positives on clean-model controls.
  • Engineered probe tooling that exposed a structural asymmetry between backdoor types: de novo backdoors collapse under strong injection while latent-amplification backdoors stay coherent. This finding directly informed the Stackelberg equilibrium computation in the associated audit-game framework.
  • Identified and characterised a late-layer attractor anomaly (layers 13–16) through activation analysis, with direct implications for choosing audit targets in sequential Stackelberg audit games.

Poisoning LLMs with a Small Dataset

Replication of the constant-number poisoning hypothesis (Souly et al., 2025)
PyTorch Data Poisoning Hugging Face
  • Designed and built a full fine-tuning and poisoning pipeline for Pythia-410m on an Apple M1 with 8 GB RAM, resolving out-of-memory and training-instability issues with the Adafactor optimizer, gradient accumulation and FP32 enforcement.
  • Implemented a stealthy backdoor with only 250 adversarial samples (<0.01% of training data): clean perplexity stayed at 32.41 while triggered perplexity spiked to 643.83.
  • Validated the attack with mechanistic token-wise loss analysis. The whole pipeline is reproducible on consumer hardware without GPU access.
